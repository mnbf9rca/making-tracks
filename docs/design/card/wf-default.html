Ghost Sign
Attraction
photo
A hand-painted advert still legible above the old shopfront, uncovered when the newer facade came down in 1998. Signs like this were painted directly onto brick by hand, often taking a week to complete. The pigment was mixed with lead white, which is why so many survive a century later. Painters were called wall dogs and worked from swing stages slung over the parapet. The lettering style dates it to the 1920s, when the shop below sold tea and coffee to the dock trade. Local historians have catalogued eleven surviving examples within a mile of here. Read more on Wikipedia ↗
