Round 2 — what the font world actually says

Research verdicts: Fraunces is 2021's branding cliché (dropped). Bricolage is the SaaS-landing-page font (dropped). New York can't legally be bundled (kept only as free fallback). Below: the research's top picks plus your Literata lean, same card as before. The italic line under each headline is the cartographic gesture — on real maps, place names of rivers and features are set in serif italic.

A

Newsreader — the research's #1 (and now my recommendation)

Editorial serif built for screens, optical sizes 6–72pt (survives Dynamic Type from tiny to AX5), and critics single out its italics — "insanely beautiful." Almost unused in the wild: no trend baggage. Guidebook, not startup.

B

Besley — the cartographic one (research runner-up)

A modern open-source Clarendon — literally the typeface family of park signage and 19th-century maps. Sturdy enough to sit over map texture. The most "outdoors heritage" of the four; pushed too bold it goes craft-brewery.

C

Piazzolla — calm small, characterful large

Critics' #1 body face of 2021: "very calm and quiet at reading sizes, but the larger it gets, the more personality it shows" — exactly how a Dynamic Type display face should behave. Slightly edgier texture than the others.

D

Literata — your lean; the safe third

First-rate craft, unimpeachably safe. The research's caveat: its excellence is optimized for long body reading, which we won't use it for — as a display voice it's restrained, and it's the ambient serif of reading apps (Google Play Books' default).
