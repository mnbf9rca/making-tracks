## §2 DS-3 inherited questions

**P2R-5 (scope persistence) — ALL scope choices persist across launches:** category selection,
include-hidden, show-saved, coverage shading — one versioned scope record (Data §11 applies),
defaults exactly as pinned (hidden OFF, saved ON, coverage ON). Scope is the user's standing
statement of intent (Product 7), not a session gesture; one row behaving differently from its
siblings is inconsistency debt. Two structural riders make persistence safe instead of surprising:

- (a) Whenever effective scope differs from default, the **Explore door wears a scope-active
  indicator** — the map may never look mysteriously thin with the reason hidden behind a door.
- (b) The picker carries a quiet **Clear-scope row** restoring defaults in one tap — persistence
  with a visible one-tap exit, nothing destroyed.

**P2R-6 (identifiers) —** The DS-3 wireframe carries an annex naming the A1-convention identifiers
for every relocated row; the contract rename happens once, in one commit, with nothing in flight.

**P2R-7 (relocated R16 proof) —** Neither Phase 2 surface pairs a filled action with an R15 state
cluster: the Scope picker's controls are states and quiet verbs; Settings/About rows carry no
filled CTA beside state pills. The proof **relocates again, explicitly**: both wireframe records
must carry the sentence *"this surface does not pair a filled action with a state cluster; the R16
proof obligation travels to the first surface that does."* The obligation may not lapse in silence.

**P2R-8 (derivation reach) — The saved-visibility filter is a DISCOVERY-MAP concern only.** It
never reaches track, list, loved, or journal derivations. Ratified as general law, generalising the
pinned `lovedPlaces()` behaviour: **scope filters govern discovery surfaces; story surfaces
(Journal: tracks, lists, loved, hidden management) are exhaustive over their own domain and NAME
overlaps instead of filtering them.** Rationale: the toggle's ruled meaning is de-cluttering
discovery; filtering saved out of lists is self-contradiction, and filtering your own history out
of tracks subtracts the story — seen is a fact (Principle 4). `Derivations.swift` stays untouched
by this filter, permanently; future reach questions answer from this law instead of case-by-case.
