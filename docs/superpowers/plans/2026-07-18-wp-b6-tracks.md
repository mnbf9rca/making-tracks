# WP-B6 — Tracks

## 1. Premise

Rob, verbatim:

> "in the past, perhaps lost in compaction, i said that we build tracks by logging where we've visited/seen. So the feature allows us to draw a track of our discoveries/journey. timebound, listbound, not sure - we need to brainstorm those sorts of filters/boundaries."

And, later the same day:

> "it's not route recording. the whole point of the app is making tracks - paths which sketch out where we've been. in my head i see that i select to view my tracks, perhaps theres a slidable time filter, and the tracks animate across the map from point to point, perhaps sharply, perhaps graceful curves, not sure. the places ping in to colour as they get touched, and the line moves on. Unvisited places are not visible. the goal of the app is to make discovering new places easy. when you go to google maps, you tap on a place, think nah not for me and go back to the map. but the place is still there. so it's hard to whittle down the map to what you want to see. in making tracks, when you find something, you save it. later, when you want to look back on your discoveries, you retrace your tracks. does this help?"

### The product this serves

The second half of that is the app's north star and it should govern every choice below. Google Maps cannot be whittled down: you dismiss a place and it is still there, so the map never becomes yours. Making Tracks inverts it. You keep what you find, and the map becomes the record of your own discovery.

**Tracks is where that record is read back.** The verb is Rob's: you **retrace your tracks**.

Five things follow.

1. The visit/seen log is the source. No separate history model, no second write path.
2. The feature draws the journey on the map, and it **animates**. Retrace is playback, not a static line. §4 is the design.
3. A slidable time filter is the primary control.
4. In retrace, **unvisited places are not visible**. The map shows only what you found.
5. The boundary and filter model is open. Rob asked for it to be brainstormed, so §7.1 presents alternatives with a recommendation, not one asserted answer.

Line character — sharp or graceful curves — is explicitly open. §4.3 argues it, because it turns out to carry more honesty weight than it looks like it does.

## 1a. Amendment — tracks are continuous

Rob, superseding part of §4:

> "dont split segments. my tracks are continuous."

**Commitments 4 and 5 below (gap threshold, burst suppression) no longer apply to the static drawn line.**
All consecutive visits connect. They are struck in place rather than deleted, so the reasoning stays
readable and nobody re-derives them.

Where the honesty burden moves: gap suppression worked by **absence**, which cannot be misread. What
survives works by **stylization** — a dashed, consistently-arced line reading as sequence rather than route.
That is weaker and it depends on the styling being honoured, so **dashed-always** and **consistent
curvature** stop being preferences and become load-bearing.

The temporal honesty moves to the **timeline axis**, which is indexed by event rather than by time: slider
positions are visits in sequence, date labels appear only where visits exist, and empty spans are not on the
axis at all. **A timeline that cannot represent empty time cannot misrepresent it.**

That is structural rather than a mechanic, which is why it is the right resting place for this burden. An
earlier revision of this amendment put it on jump cuts in the replay; those are superseded, because an
event-indexed axis has no gaps to jump. Autoplay still advances **by event**, one beat per discovery — a
replay that moved faster over a long gap would claim travel speed.

The product reason: a split track is not a track. Continuity is what makes it one.

## 2. Spec delta — read this before anything else

Issue #17 and the spec's own WP-B6 entry are faithful transcriptions of the design spec. Their written scope is a **chronological list**: visits ordered by `visited_at`, a Loved filter, verdict editing from the visit row, snapshot-anchored rendering.

Rob wants a **drawn track on the map**. That is a second rendering of the same data on a different surface, and it goes beyond the written spec in four separate ways.

**A drawn line is not merely unspecified. It is fenced off twice.**

- §1, naming risk: "'tracks' reads as GPX/route recording. The subtitle and first screenshot must immediately establish this is *discovery*, not route logging." This is stated as a constraint on App Store packaging.
- §10, out of scope: route planning, "(also: the app must never *look* like a route recorder — see naming risk)."

Zero mentions of a line, route, path or polyline anywhere in the spec. The only Tracks-on-map coupling the spec defines is pin fading (§3.2 pin matrix, §5.1 data-driven opacity). Where §1 says "everything, tracks and all" it means faded pins.

Worth being precise about the grounds. The prohibition is about **appearance and store positioning**, not privacy and not the data. §3.4 already guarantees no auto-marking from geolocation, and nothing here changes that. The spec's architecture poses no obstacle either: §5.1's mandated `MLNShapeSource` plus style-layer substrate renders a `LineString` as naturally as points.

**Rob's vision statement answers this in substance.** *"it's not route recording. the whole point of the app is making tracks - paths which sketch out where we've been."* The §1 constraint was written to stop the product being mistaken for a GPX logger. It was not written to stop the app drawing the thing it is named after. On that reading the fence guards the framing, and the framing is now stated: a sketch of where you have been, read back as a retrace.

**The formal amendment is still Rob's to make**, and this document does not make it. What it does is design to the vision and show the work: §4 argues that an animated, event-paced, discovery-first retrace is not merely permitted by the §1 constraint but is a stronger answer to it than a static line would be, because motion moves the emphasis from *path* to *sequence*.

If the amendment is refused, §9's build order still ships items 1–3 unchanged.

The three smaller deltas:

- **A time filter.** Zero spec mentions. §3.1 anticipates timestamp comparison for future per-list freshness queries but specifies no date-range filter. Nothing to overturn here, only something to fill.
- **A category filter on Tracks.** §3.3 grants Tracks exactly one filter axis, Loved. Reusing the shipped pin category filter is new scope.
- **New rendering rules with no spec basis**: gap suppression, burst handling, event pacing, the retrace animation itself. §4 and §5.

## 3. Recommended design

### 3.1 Shape

**There is no Track object.** A track is a rendering of the visit log under the filters currently in effect: time range, category, Loved, and always the hidden-places exclusion. Change a filter and the line recomposes.

Nothing new is stored for the default behaviour. No `tracks` table, no `trip_id`, no `segment_index`, no stored polyline. The feature is one query plus one map layer.

The argument for deriving rather than storing:

- Nothing to name, nothing to start, nothing to forget. A user who never sets anything up still has a track. Forgetting to press record is the most common failure of every trip logger, and it is removed structurally rather than by nagging.
- A wrong boundary hides nothing. The log is always fully present, so no user action can corrupt or lose a discovery.
- No inference about the user's life is computed or persisted. See §6.
- Retroactive edits are free. A filter is not a migration.

### 3.2 What was grafted, and from where

The winning fork was `no-boundary`. Judges found four real defects in it. Each is fixed below with an idea taken from a runner-up. Named explicitly so nothing is laundered.

| Graft | From | What it fixes |
|---|---|---|
| Never draw a connector across a gap above a threshold | `gap-session`, `time-bound` | The transcontinental straight line and the across-a-month line are never generated, rather than styled down. Absence reads correctly with no legend. |
| Burst suppression: visits recorded within a short window render as an unordered cluster, not a directed segment | `gap-session` honesty review | Batch entry (log the day's places at dinner) otherwise produces the app's most confident line across the path it knows least about. |
| Activity clusters offered as one-tap date ranges | `gap-session` proposals, `manual` retroactive selection | Fixes the winner's worst usability flaw: "show me Lisbon" needed a manual pan plus date configuration. Now it is a tap. Nothing is auto-applied. |
| Viewport is camera, never a SQL predicate | `time-bound` | The winner made the viewport a hard filter, which detaches the line at the screen edge on every pan and makes its contiguity signal fire constantly. Removed. |
| Hidden-places exclusion lives in the SQL, with a teeth test | `time-bound`, `gap-session` | `setHidden` never touches `visits`, so any reader that forgets the join un-hides a place on the map, in a line, with a timestamp. |
| Recipient's own track on a received list | `list-bound` | If list sharing ever ships, a received list can draw the *recipient's* progress. Nothing crosses the wire. |
| Time is droppable on egress | `time-bound` | Order and absolute time are separable. Makes any future sharing amendment arguable instead of a flat contradiction. |
| Fix the destructive un-see path | `manual` | `deleteVisits(placeID:)` wipes every visit row for a place. Already wrong today, independent of Tracks. |
| Tie-break on rowid, never spatially | `list-bound`, `gap-session` | Spatial tie-breaking silently invents the most plausible-looking path. It is dangerous precisely because the output looks better. |
| Solid stroke reserved permanently for a genuinely recorded trace | all four runners-up | Costs nothing now, gives the product a provenance grammar before there is anything to distinguish. |

### 3.3 Data model

Nothing new stored. The query:

```sql
SELECT v.id, v.place_id, v.visited_at, v.verdict,
       s.name, s.lat, s.lon, s.category, s.tier
FROM visits v
JOIN place_snapshots s ON s.place_id = v.place_id
WHERE v.place_id NOT IN (SELECT place_id FROM hidden_places)
  AND v.visited_at >= :from AND v.visited_at < :to
  AND (:categoryFilterOff OR s.category IN (...))
  AND (:lovedOnly = 0 OR EXISTS (
        SELECT 1 FROM visits lv
        WHERE lv.place_id = v.place_id AND lv.verdict = 'loved'))
ORDER BY v.visited_at ASC, v.id ASC;
```

Notes, each load-bearing.

- The join is total by construction. `snapshotIfNeeded` (`ios/Sources/MakingTracksData/Interactions.swift:5-25`) runs inside the same write as every `recordVisit` (`Interactions.swift:30`), is write-once, and nothing in the tree deletes from `place_snapshots`. `lat`/`lon` are NOT NULL and range-validated in `PlaceRef.init` (`Models/PlaceRef.swift:40-44`). Tile eviction (`pruneLocked`, `MakingTracksTiles.swift:1014`) touches downloaded bytes only. Geometry survives offline and outlives the tiles.
- The `hidden_places` clause is mandatory, not defensive. `setHidden` (`Interactions.swift:112-131`) leaves every visit row intact.
- Ordering is `(visited_at, id)`. GRDB writes `.datetime` as `"yyyy-MM-dd HH:mm:ss.SSS"` in UTC, so lexicographic order equals chronological order. `id` is insertion order and is the only honest tie-break.
- `lovedOnly` is per-place, matching `viewportState`'s aggregate (`Derivations.swift:107-117`). It has to be: `setLoved` writes the verdict onto only the newest visit row while unlove clears every row (`Interactions.swift:87-110`). Consequence, stated plainly: a Loved-filtered track cannot say which visit was the loved one, so no per-visit heart on the line.
- New helper in `ios/Sources/MakingTracksData/Derivations.swift`. It would be the first query in the codebase that orders by time and the first that joins `visits` to `place_snapshots`. Today `visited_at` is effectively write-only, read only by the `LIMIT 1` subquery in `setLoved` (`Interactions.swift:97`).

**Index.** `idx_visits_place` (`Migrations.swift:22`) does not serve a time range scan. At current magnitudes (no caps, no pruning, hundreds to low thousands of rows) a scan is fine. Adding `idx_visits_visited_at` means migration `v3`, and the guard at `Migrations.swift:83-90` throws `databaseFromNewerAppVersion` on unknown identifiers, so a v3 database is refused by any older build. That is a one-way door for a performance fix that is not yet needed. Recommendation: ship without it, revisit when there is a measured problem.

**What the schema cannot do, and must not be faked.**

- `visited_at` is record time, not visit time. `recordVisit` computes `now()` once and writes it to both `visited_at` and `created_at` (`Interactions.swift:31-38`). No backdating, no edit UI. "I saw this yesterday" is unrepresentable.
- No timezone or UTC offset is stored anywhere. Local wall-clock and local day boundaries are unreconstructable for past visits.
- No first/last seen pair, no visit note, no session column.

### 3.4 A defect in shipped code this depends on

`setVisited(place, false)` routes to `deleteVisits(placeID:)`, which runs `DELETE FROM visits WHERE place_id = ?` (`Interactions.swift:50-57`). Every row for that place, not the one being un-seen. The row-scoped `deleteVisit(id:)` exists at `Interactions.swift:44-48` and no product code calls it.

This is already wrong. It destroys the re-visit history that §3.1's event-log model exists to preserve. Tracks makes it visible: one tap silently removes a point from a drawn line. **Fix it as its own commit with its own test, before or alongside B6.**

### 3.5 Surfaces

**Map.** §1 already specifies a two-pole on-map control, "Fresh snow" / "My tracks". The second pole gains meaning: it draws the line as well as showing faded pins. No new navigation, and the metaphor the spec already chose does the teaching.

A slim readout describes the current view, never a trip. It states what is on screen and what the filters excluded, in plain words rather than a second visual language.

**Filter strip.** One row of chips: time range, categories (reusing the shipped pin filter predicate), Loved. They compose. There is no chip that produces "a track" versus "another track". One log, several lenses.

**Activity clusters.** The Tracks screen offers the user's dense runs of activity as one-tap date ranges. Tapping one sets the time chip and fits the camera. This is a suggestion the user accepts, computed from gaps in their own log and shown with the gap that produced it. Nothing is applied silently, nothing is hidden, and declining costs one tap. Whether these are also nameable is an open choice (§7).

**Tracks screen.** A new `MenuDestination` case (`MapScreen.swift:263-268`, routed at `:2015-2042`, row in `AppMenuRootView` at `:2069-2100`). It reads the same query with the same predicate. Rows show place, category, date, heart. Verdict is editable inline, which §3.3 already sanctions. Note `case .lists` still renders `ListsPlaceholderView()` (`:2019`, defined `:2128`), so Tracks would land beside a stub.

Map and list stay bound. Change a filter in one, the other changes.

**Fit to bounds.** Offered as an explicit action, not applied on open. The viewport is not a filter, so auto-fitting is not required for correctness, and choosing the user's camera for them on every filter change is worse than letting them ask.

## 4. Retrace — the animated playback

### 4.1 What it is

You open Tracks, and the map replays your discoveries in the order you made them. The line advances point to point. Each place pings into colour as the line reaches it. Places you never found are not on the map at all.

The controls are a time slider and the filter chips. Move the slider and the replay re-scopes.

Four properties, each doing real work:

**Unvisited places are invisible in retrace.** This is the strongest setting of the pin matrix the spec already defines (§3.2, §5.1 data-driven opacity), and it is the whole product thesis made literal: the map is whittled down to what you kept. It is also an honesty win, because a map showing only your discoveries makes no claim about what you walked past.

**Places ping into colour as the line touches them.** The discoveries are the events. Colour arriving on a pin is the moment of finding something, replayed. This puts the emphasis on the points, which are real recorded data, and leaves the line as connective tissue between them.

**The line moves on.** It is a gesture, not an artifact. A static line sits on the map as a claim to be read; a line that arrives and passes is a narration of order.

**Pace by event, not by elapsed time or distance.** One beat per discovery, regardless of whether the next was ten minutes or ten days later, ten metres or ten miles. This is the single most important honesty decision in the animation, and §4.2 explains why.

### 4.2 The implied-path problem, and what animation does to it

We have discrete taps with coordinates. We have no observation of where the user was between two taps.

Every visit row is an explicit action. Either the "Seen" button on the place card (`MapScreen.swift:3163-3170`, through `setVisited` at `:3225-3228`) or the "Seen it" tap on the nearby prompt (`MapScreen.swift:1461-1490`, writing at `:1585-1594`). Proximity proposes: `NearbyPromptSelector.candidate` (`Sources/MakingTracksCore/NearbyPromptSelector.swift:17-55`) runs a haversine against the live coordinate within 125 m. No row is written without a tap. There is no geofencing, no background location, and `Info.plist` declares only `NSLocationWhenInUseUsageDescription` (`App/project.yml:89`).

A line between two of those points asserts something we did not record. This is a known failure and the evidence is in competitors' support docs. Polarsteps ships an article titled "What to do if you see an incorrect line in your trip?". FindPenguins ships "There is no route line on my maps". Users read a line as a factual claim about the path taken and file bugs when it is not one. Apps whose data is genuinely discrete points draw no line at all: Apple Photos Places, Swarm, Day One.

**Does animating it make this better or worse?** Both, and which wins is decided by two choices.

**Motion helps, because it foregrounds the thing we actually know.** A static line is a spatial claim: this path exists. An animated line is a temporal claim: these happened in this order. Order is exactly what the timestamps give us; the geometry between points is exactly what they do not. Moving the emphasis from shape to sequence moves it from the unrecorded to the recorded. The ping-into-colour reinforces it: the events are the arrivals, not the travel.

**Motion hurts if it imitates a vehicle.** A dot gliding smoothly along a path is the navigation idiom — Google's route preview, Strava's flyover. Both depict a journey that was measured. If our replay looks like that, the animation asserts a trajectory more vividly than a static line ever could, because it shows something *moving through* the space between points.

The two choices that decide it:

**a. Pace by event, never by time or distance.** If the line moves faster over long gaps, it claims travel speed. If it takes a week of real elapsed time proportionally longer, it claims duration. Both are claims about the journey we did not record. One beat per discovery says "then this, then this" — a sequence, which is all we have. This also makes the replay watchable: a trip with one gap of six months does not stall.

**b. Express gaps as a cut, not a stretch.** §4.4's rule is that no connector is drawn above a gap threshold. In an animated form that becomes better, not worse: the replay simply cuts to the next cluster. A jump cut is instantly legible as "and then, elsewhere" with no legend and no second dash grammar. Film taught everyone this vocabulary decades ago. **Animation gives us a better way to express absence than static rendering has** — this is the clearest gain from Rob's vision.

Net assessment: **animation is a net gain for honesty, conditional on (a) and (b).** Without them it is a net loss and worse than a static line. Neither is a detail to be settled in implementation.

### 4.3 Line character — sharp or curved

Rob left this open. It carries more weight than it appears to.

The intuition is that straight is honest and curves are decorative. The opposite is closer to true.

A **straight** segment between two places can be mistaken for a real path, because plenty of real paths are straight. It is a plausible claim, and plausible-but-false is the dangerous kind.

A **consistent arc** cannot be mistaken for anything. It is the airline-route-map idiom, and nobody has ever thought the plane flew that curve. The arc reads as *abstract connection* precisely because it is obviously stylized. It says these two things are linked, in this order, without proposing a route.

So the recommendation is **a consistent, shallow arc** — the same curvature rule for every segment, never fitted to terrain, never varying with distance in a way that could encode anything. Curvature that varied would start encoding, and anything that encodes is a claim.

What must not ship, whichever is chosen: curves that follow roads or coastlines, per-segment curvature variation, or any easing that reads as acceleration and braking.

This stays open for Rob (§7.3). Both options are compatible with everything else here; the arc is the safer of the two and it is also the one he called "graceful".

### 4.4 Commitments

Seven, unchanged in substance by the animation and re-checked against it. Each is either a refusal or a borrowed convention.

**1. Never snap to roads.** Snapping converts "I do not know the path" into a specific false statement about which road was taken. Google's snapping is defensible because it disambiguates a dense GPS trace. Between two check-ins it is invention. Not a setting, not for an export.

**2. Dashed, always.** `lineDashPattern` on `MLNLineStyleLayer`. Dashes are the settled cartographic sign for a linear feature that is not really there. The USGS standard is a direct fit: solid means located, dash means approximately located, dotted means concealed.

**3. Solid is reserved permanently for a genuinely recorded trace.** It costs nothing today. If GPS logging ever ships, solid versus dashed is already a provenance signal the user reads at a glance, with no redesign and no re-education.

**4. ~~No connector above a gap threshold.~~ SUPERSEDED — see §1a.** ~~Two points far apart in time get nothing drawn between them. The absence is the honest statement and the eye reads it correctly without a legend.~~ Overturned by owner ruling: all consecutive visits connect. The absence signal moves to the animation's jump cuts.

**5. ~~Burst suppression.~~ SUPERSEDED — see §1a.** ~~Visits recorded within a short window are rendered as a cluster, not as a directed sequence. Tap order is not travel order.~~ Overturned by owner ruling. Note the underlying concern is real and now unaddressed in the drawing: batch-logged visits still produce a confident line across a path the app knows least about. #221's reordering is what answers it — once a user can supply the true order, the line reflects intent rather than tap sequence.

**6. Points dominate, line recedes.** Thin, desaturated, lower opacity, beneath the pins. The discoveries are the data. The connector is scaffolding. This inverts the navigation hierarchy where the route is the hero, and it is the single cheapest thing that removes the "recommendation" read.

**7. No navigation blue.** It is the universal follow-this signal.

**Direction.** Recommendation: do not assert it in v1. Chevrons and an early-to-late colour ramp both make an explicit claim about direction of travel derived from record-time timestamps. Given commitment 5, that claim is unsafe for exactly the users the feature is aimed at. Left open in §7. Note also that `line-gradient` requires line-distance metrics on the source and does not compose with a dash array, so a ramp and dashing are not both available on one layer.

**Filtered gaps.** Filtering removes intervening visits, which stretches a longer segment between the survivors. That is a bigger claim than the one we started with. Two options, both compatible with the above: suppress the connector across a filtered gap under the same rule as commitment 4, or state the omission in plain words in the readout ("3 places hidden by your filters"). Recommendation: the plain-words readout. A second dash grammar teaches cartographic provenance to someone on holiday, without a legend, and they will read it as a rendering glitch.

**Hidden places are the exception, deliberately.** Where a hidden place sits between two visible ones, the line bridges silently with no gap marker. A "something is hidden here" affordance would re-expose exactly what hiding removed. This is a conscious asymmetry with the filtered-gap rule and it should be documented in code, not discovered later.

**Why a filter-derived line is the safest version.** It moves. Tick a chip and it reshapes. A recorded route does not do that. The instability is itself a signal, and no fork that persists a trip object gets it, because a persisted trip renders identically forever and therefore reads as a record of a path.

## 5. Rendering and feasibility

**Pins today are not annotations.** There is no `MLNAnnotation`, `MLNPolyline` or `MLNPointFeature` anywhere in `ios/`. A single `MLNShapeSource` ("pins", `PinLayers.sourceID`, `ios/Sources/MakingTracksMapStyle/PinLayers.swift:6`) feeds four style layers, created empty at `ios/App/Sources/Map/MLNMapViewRepresentable.swift:252-254` and installed in `mapView(_:didFinishLoading:)` at `:246`. MapLibre is pinned at 6.27.0 (`project.xcworkspace/xcshareddata/swiftpm/Package.resolved:14-22`).

**The track needs its own source.** The pins source is viewport-scoped and replaced wholesale on every refresh (`updateSource(on:features:)`, `:294-325`). A track cannot be viewport-scoped or the line breaks at the screen edge.

Work items:

1. A `LineString` builder in `ios/Sources/MakingTracksMapStyle/FeatureEncoding.swift` (existing point builders at `:23`, `:38`). One feature per segment, carrying a gap property so suppression rules can filter.
2. A `TrackLayers` enum beside `PinLayers.swift`. Note that module has no MapLibre dependency and emits `JSONValue`, so it is a declarative style spec only.
3. Source and `MLNLineStyleLayer` created in the imperative `didFinishLoading` block at `:246`, placed with `style.insertLayer(_:below:)` against `"pins-circle"`. MapLibre draws in add order, so appending after `pins-icon` paints over the icons. `PinLayers.pinLayers()` (`PinLayers.swift:118`) is a test mirror, not the install path.
4. Cache track geometry on the Coordinator like `pendingFeatures` (`:161`) and re-apply from `didFinishLoading`. A style reload destroys all runtime layers, and four themes ship, so reloads are routine.
5. An explicit change guard so the source updates only when the track changes, in the manner of `currentVisibleCategories` (`:328`). The `JSONValue` to String to Data to `MLNShape(data:encoding:)` round trip at `:303-310` runs on the main actor and currently fires on every non-reload `updateUIView`, meaning every camera idle. Build the shape off the main actor and hand it across.
6. Camera. There is no fit-bounds code. All camera control is `setCenter(_:zoomLevel:animated:)` (`:88`, `:120`). Extend `ViewportCameraRequest` (`MapScreen.swift:258`) with a bounds case driving `setVisibleCoordinateBounds(_:edgePadding:animated:)`, reusing the request-ID dedupe at `:224` or SwiftUI re-renders will re-animate.
7. Outward routing. Routing a selection from the menu sheet to the map has no existing path. `AppShellModel.deepLinkPath` (`:308-311`) routes inward. The sheet already writes shell state outward (`MapScreen.swift:1191`), so this is small, but it is new.

**Dash patterns are not data-driven.** `lineDashPattern` is a per-layer constant in MapLibre native. Per-segment dash variation requires N layers with N predicates over one source, each re-added on style reload. Recommendation: one dash pattern, and use suppression rather than variation to express uncertainty. That is why commitment 4 exists and per-segment confidence decay does not.

**Contrast.** All four `MapTheme` presets are light paper, backgrounds `#F1EDDF` to `#F4F1EA` (`ios/Sources/MakingTracksMapStyle/PaperStyle.swift:22-143`). No dark theme, and the map does not follow `colorScheme`. Pin colour is a theme-independent constant, `PinLayers.pinColor = "#E4572E"` (`PinLayers.swift:4`), and it is roughly 3.4:1 against paper. It would not clear the 4.5:1 AA commitment (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-07-17-wp-regions-model.md:240`) as a thin line. The track needs a darker hue or a two-layer casing, which also helps where the line crosses the basemap's own road layers (`PaperStyle.swift:254-261`). That AA figure is a plan commitment with nothing enforcing it. WP-LOGGING (commit `969df3d`) is the precedent for turning a commitment into a check.

**Performance.** GPU cost of one line layer is negligible. The cost is main-actor JSON. Volumes are small: `visits` grows one row per Seen tap with no caps and no pruning, and the one-tap-per-place UI keeps it close to one row per distinct place.

**Concurrency.** `AppDatabase` is `Sendable`, reads are serialised on a `DatabaseQueue`, and the existing map path already dispatches via `Task.detached` (`MapScreen.swift:3690`). The query runs off the main actor. Only the shape hand-off touches it.

**Testing.** The hidden-places exclusion needs a test with teeth: hide a place mid-track, assert it is absent from both the list and the geometry, then neuter the WHERE clause and confirm the test goes red. The WP-LOGGING privacy lint (`969df3d`) applies unchanged to all new files from the first commit: no place ids, coordinates or timestamps in public `os.Logger` fields.

## 6. Privacy

**On-device is settled.** Everything the feature touches (`visits`, `place_snapshots`, `hidden_places`) is already in the one GRDB database inside the iOS sandbox, which the threat model deems sufficient isolation. The recommended design stores nothing new, adds no sensor, no network call, no worker, no entitlement, and no permission string. §5.2's "no runtime compute anywhere in v1" is preserved. No defence is proposed here that only matters once the sandbox is broken.

**Hidden places are the live regression risk.** Hiding does not delete visits. Any new reader that forgets the join un-hides a place, on the map, in a line, with a timestamp. That is the most legible possible privacy regression and it is why the exclusion sits in the SQL rather than in a caller.

**Inference: none, deliberately.** The standard segmentation technique is stay-point detection with dwell-time and distance thresholds. Applied to a discovery log, that is a home, work and sleep detector: the gaps it keys on are overnights and returns to base. The literature is candid that these are rule-based, threshold-tuned and lack universality. Manufacturing a new derived fact about the user from the most sensitive data the app holds is a §9 question, not an implementation detail. The recommended design does not compute it. The activity clusters in §3.5 are presented as gaps in the user's own log with the gap shown, and they never regroup, hide or persist anything without a tap.

**Sharing is out of v1.** Stated plainly, and it is not a close call.

- §10 excludes sharing from v1.
- §5 states user data never leaves the device in v1.
- §9's v2 account stores "only the sharing identity and the lists you chose to share — never visits, map, or history". The spec's own v2 sharing design structurally cannot carry a track.
- privacy.md commits that a shared list "does NOT say whether you've visited any of the places, or what you loved". A drawn track inverts that sentence three ways at once: visited-ness, loved-ness, and sequence on top.
- privacy.md principle 6 reads "We do not track where users are. The app builds a trail of where you've been, but that trail is private to you." That sanctions the trail as a concept and fences it in the same sentence.

§1 calls Tracks "the shareable artefact and the retention mechanism". That phrase has no mechanism and no privacy analysis behind it, and it is contradicted by §5, §9 and §10. **It must not be read as pre-approval.**

So: no share sheet, no export, no poster, no save-as-image, no GPX or GeoJSON. A screenshot remains egress the app cannot control, and nothing in this design should be described as a control against it.

**What an amendment would have to say, if sharing is ever wanted.** Flagged for Rob's ratification. Not asserted, not assumed, and deliberately not written as policy text here.

1. Whether visit history may ever appear in a shared payload. §9 currently excludes it in terms.
2. Whether track visibility and location visibility are separate permissions. Polarsteps ships trip visibility and live-location visibility as independent toggles. Collapsing them is the failure this design would otherwise walk into.
3. Whether every new render or export surface inherits redaction by default rather than by remembering to. Strava's rule for Flyover, which respects map visibility and privacy zones without the user opting in.

Two design properties that make such an amendment arguable rather than a flat contradiction, both free in this fork:

- **There is no Track object.** Nothing has an identity to name, retrieve or attach a share sheet to. This is a rhetorical shield, not a mechanism. Egress is prevented by the absence of an egress path, not by the absence of a row, and that should be said honestly.
- **Time is droppable.** A track is a query, so a redacted form that keeps order and drops absolute timestamps is a projection of the same query, not a second data model.

**Recipient's track.** If list sharing ships in v2, a received list can draw a track through the places *the recipient* has visited. It leaks nothing in either direction, and the sender's visits never cross the wire. This is the one place where Tracks and sharing compose safely, and it is worth writing down now so nobody later reaches for the unsafe version.

**Build order.** The rendering work and any sharing work must not ship in the same gate.

**Retention.** Nothing prunes any user-data table today. No caps, no TTL, no VACUUM. The only `prune` in the tree is `pruneLocked` in the tile store, which bounds downloaded bytes. This design adds no rows and no new retention posture. Whether a movement trace should have one is a policy question, open in §7.

## 7. Open choices for Rob

This is the part Rob asked to brainstorm. Recommendations are given with the tradeoff, not asserted.

### 7.1 The boundary model — the main decision

Five genuine alternatives. They are not mutually exclusive; the first can absorb parts of the others.

**A. No boundary. Filters only.** Recommended. One log, several lenses, nothing stored, nothing named, nothing to forget. Tradeoff: retrieval is composed rather than stored, so "show me Lisbon" needs a filter set rather than a tap. The activity clusters in §3.5 close most of that gap. Weakest on the souvenir feeling: there is nothing named to be proud of.

**B. Derived gap sessions.** Walk the log, cut where the gap is large, show every boundary with the evidence that produced it, one tap to join or split. Tradeoff: every threshold is an expert guess applied to every user in every city, and the gaps are gaps between *taps*, not visits. Polarsteps, the best-resourced player in this space, looked at auto-detection and shipped manual trips instead. Also carries the inference cost in §6.

**C. Manual start and stop.** The user opens a track, visits land in it, they close it. Zero inference, zero thresholds. Tradeoff: forgetting to start is the default outcome, and the failure is silent — the user arrives home to an empty screen with no indication that anything could have been drawn. It is also the most route-recorder-shaped option, and Start/Stop are Strava's verbs, which aims straight at the §1/§10 constraint.

**D. List-bound.** A track through the visited members of a list. The user already drew the boundary, no inference, and it is the only option that needs no new index and no migration. Tradeoff: lists are curated by theme, not by trip, so a track through "Date nights" spanning three years is a line but not a journey. Worse, the discovery-first user who taps Seen and never saves anything gets no track at all, ever. And it welds the trace to the one object the product intends to share.

**E. Time-bound spans.** Named date ranges evaluated live, nesting and overlapping freely. Tradeoff: makes wall-clock time a first-class user-visible fact on a column that holds record time, and needs a stored UTC offset going forward that cannot repair a single existing row.

**Recommendation: A, with the cluster affordance from B, and D's recipient-track idea reserved for whenever list sharing lands.** A is the only option that is free at the storage layer, computes no inference, and cannot lose a user's trip through a setup step they did not know about.

### 7.2 Whether the line ships at all

The §1/§10 amendment. Rob's call, not a design call. If refused, WP-B6 ships as issue #17 wrote it: the chronological list, the Loved filter, verdict editing, no map geometry. Everything in §4 and §5 is then deferred, not discarded.

### 7.3 Thresholds and defaults — all open

- Gap above which no connector is drawn — in retrace, the gap at which the replay cuts.
- Burst window within which visits render as a cluster rather than a sequence.
- **Line character: consistent shallow arc, or straight segments.** §4.3 recommends the arc and argues why it is the safer of the two.
- **Beat duration** — the pace of one discovery to the next, and whether it accelerates when a replay is long.
- **Whether retrace autoplays on open or waits for a tap.** Autoplay is the better first impression; a tap is the better repeat experience.
- **Whether the replay can be scrubbed** with the time slider while running, and what the slider's handle means during playback.
- **What the map does during replay** — follow the line, or hold a fitted view of the whole range. Following is more cinematic and hides context; holding is calmer and may leave the action tiny.
- **Ping treatment** — colour arrival only, or scale and colour. Must not read as a notification badge.
- **Whether the line persists after the replay ends**, or fades and leaves only the coloured pins.
- Default time range on first open. This decides what a user sees the first time they open Tracks after a holiday, so it matters more than its size suggests.
- Minimum visit count below which no line is drawn at all.
- Dash and gap lengths, and whether they scale with zoom.
- Track line colour and whether a casing is used. Must clear 4.5:1 against all four paper backgrounds. `#E4572E` does not.
- Line width and opacity.
- Camera edge padding, and the widest bounds a fit will zoom to before it refuses.
- Maximum points rendered before geometry degrades.

### 7.4 Smaller open questions

- Whether direction is shown at all. Recommendation: not in v1, per §4.
- Whether filtered gaps are stated in the readout or expressed visually. Recommendation: the readout.
- Whether activity clusters can be named, and where a name would live. Naming implies a stored object, which reopens most of §7.1.
- Whether track display persists across launches.
- Whether the system "Want to go" list is eligible for anything here.
- Whether to add `idx_visits_visited_at` now or wait for a measured problem. Recommendation: wait. Migration `v3` is a one-way door on any device that opens it (`Migrations.swift:83-90`).
- Whether tracks should ever have a retention policy. Nothing in the app has one today.
- The on-screen wording for the "My tracks" pole, given the naming risk.

## 8. Alternatives considered and why they lost

Recorded so nobody re-derives them. Scores are from the four-lens review (tourist, privacy, engineering, honesty).

**Gap sessions — 26.** Derive trips by cutting the log where the time gap is large, show the evidence, let the user join or split. Strongest structural honesty idea in the set: no line is ever drawn across a boundary, so the long false segments are never generated. Lost on three counts. The gaps are between taps, not visits, so batch entry produces one high-confidence session whose line zigzags across the map, and nothing in the schema can detect it. That also makes the sessioniser untestable, since there is no ground truth to grade it against, leaving its thresholds permanently unfalsifiable. And the UI showed its own arithmetic to the user: gap chips, provisional dividers, "joined by you" markers, two dash grammars. That is a debugging view shipped as a product. Its boundary-as-render-gate idea was grafted.

**Manual start and stop — 25.** The user declares a track and visits land in it while it is open. Best possible answer on inference: zero sensor data, zero derived facts, no thresholds to be wrong about. Lost because the entry cost is paid before any reward exists, and the failure is silent. The modal traveller never taps Start, arrives home to an empty screen, and is never told the app could have drawn their week. Its own weaknesses section conceded the retroactive multi-select rescue becomes the real primary flow, which is more work than the thing it replaced. Separately, it is the most route-recorder-shaped option, and dashing does not change the verb on the button. Its retroactive-selection idea and its un-see fix were grafted.

**List-bound — 24.5.** A track through the visited members of a list. Cheapest option at the storage layer by a distance, and it was the only fork with no new index and no migration. Lost on the shape of the boundary. Lists are themed and permanent; trips are bounded and specific. List membership is an intention recorded before the trip, while a track is a memory formed during it, so the discovery-first user who taps Seen and saves nothing gets nothing, forever, with no in-app signal. It also welds the movement trace to `lists`, the one object designated for egress, which puts the most sensitive derived artefact one join away from the share payload and makes the "sharing a list never shares your track" invariant a permanent discipline problem rather than a structural fact. Its recipient-track idea and its tie-break rule were grafted.

**Time-bound spans — 23.** Named date ranges evaluated live over the log, nesting and overlapping freely. Best rendering craft in the set, including the gap-suppression rule and the confidence-decay idea. Lost because it makes wall-clock time the organising axis of the whole product on the weakest column in the schema. Its honesty machinery was calibrated on the false signal: confidence decay drew the most solid, most confident connectors for exactly the batch logger it knew least about. It also proposed a two-handle histogram scrubber for a log the app may rarely have enough data to fill, and its `track_spans` rows outlive the visits they describe, so un-seeing everything would leave a named period of movement behind. Its gap-suppression rule, its droppable-time-on-egress idea, and its SQL-level hidden-places exclusion were grafted.

## 9. What ships, and in what order

1. Fix `deleteVisits(placeID:)` on the un-see path. Own commit, own test.
2. The track query in `Derivations.swift`, with the hidden-places exclusion in SQL and a teeth test.
3. The Tracks screen: chronological list, Loved filter, verdict editing. This is issue #17's written scope and it ships whether or not the line is approved.
4. The map layer: own shape source, dashed connector, continuous across all consecutive visits (§1a), points dominant, contrast-checked colour. Static first — the geometry has to be right before it moves.
5. The retrace animation on top of it: event-paced advance, ping-into-colour on arrival, cut across gaps, unvisited places hidden in this mode.
6. The time slider and the activity clusters.
7. Fit-to-bounds camera and the outward routing channel.

No sharing work in any of these.
